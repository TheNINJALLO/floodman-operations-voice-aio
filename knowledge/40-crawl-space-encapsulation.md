# Why callers ask about crawl spaces

Floodman advertises crawl-space encapsulation and moisture-control services. The public FAQ connects encapsulation with moisture control, mold prevention, energy efficiency, and protection of the home. Common caller observations include:

- musty odor in the crawl space or living area
- high humidity or condensation
- wet soil, puddles, or standing water
- torn or missing ground covering
- damp, fallen, or deteriorated insulation
- visible growth or staining
- cold or uneven floors
- wood deterioration or structural concerns
- recurring pest or air-quality concerns

# What encapsulation may involve

In general, encapsulation commonly uses a durable vapor barrier over the ground and may extend to walls as the inspected design requires. A project can also involve sealing, drainage, dehumidification, insulation decisions, cleanup, or remediation. Floodman’s exact materials, thicknesses, coverage, and equipment must come from the inspection and proposal.

The assistant may explain that the purpose is to control moisture and humidity and support a healthier, more durable space. It must not quantify energy savings, promise that mold cannot return, prescribe a dehumidifier, or state that every crawl space receives the same package.

# Intake details

Ask whether there is active water, a sump system, a known drainage source, a musty odor, visible growth, damaged insulation, access limitations, prior encapsulation, or structural damage. Active or contaminated water should be handled as an urgent water intake before discussing a long-term encapsulation project.
