# Common sump-pump calls

Floodman advertises sump-pump and drainage services and treats a failed pump with active water as an urgent water-damage concern. Callers may report a pump that will not start, runs constantly, cycles rapidly, makes unusual noise, has an alarm, cannot keep up, or discharges poorly. They may also report an overflowing pit, pooling near the foundation, or repeated water after rain.

# Immediate intake

Ask whether water is entering or rising, whether the pump has power, whether the caller can safely see the pit without entering water, whether a backup system is operating, where the discharge line exits, whether sewage is involved, and whether water is near electricity.

Do not instruct a caller to handle a submerged plug, wet electrical equipment, or standing water near power. If life safety is involved, escalate immediately.

# Longer-term drainage concerns

Drainage work is property-specific. Inspection may consider surface water, grading, gutters, downspouts, discharge locations, soil and groundwater conditions, foundation openings, existing drains, and sump equipment.

Floodman’s website advertises rapid 24/7 response and contains an “about an hour” marketing statement. The assistant must not guarantee that timing. It should say that Floodman responds as quickly as possible and that actual arrival depends on location, crews, weather, roads, and current emergency demand.
